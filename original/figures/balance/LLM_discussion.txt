4. Actionable Recommendations

Here is what I suggest you do next, based on this statistical evidence:

    Use a Log Transform for Regression: This is the most critical step. To handle the strong right-skew, you should not predict distance. You should predict log(distance). This transformation will "pull in" the long tail, making the target variable much more "normal" and easier for models to learn. You can simply take the exp() of the model's prediction to get back to the real distance.

    Make ship_type a Core Feature: You must include ship_type in your model. For most ML models, you will want to one-hot encode this categorical feature.

    Use Stratified Splitting: When you create your training and test sets, you must stratify by ship_type. If you do a simple random split, you risk getting zero examples of a rare type (like Sailing Vessel or Pilot Vessel ) in your test set, and you will have no idea how your model performs on them.

Choose Your Model Wisely:

    Regression: Tree-based models (like Random Forest or Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)) will likely outperform linear models because they can naturally handle the different behaviors of each ship type without complex feature engineering.

    Classification: Use models that handle imbalance well (e.g., Random Forest, XGBoost with scale_pos_weight parameter) and focus on Precision-Recall curves (AUC-PR), not just F1-score.

Consider Model-per-Type (Advanced): An advanced (and likely very effective) approach would be to train 14 different, simpler regression models—one for each ship type. When you need a prediction, you first check the ship type and then use its dedicated model.
